# 02 — What Is Research?

> **A 5-minute explanation of what "doing research" really means.**

---

## 🎯 In One Sentence

**Research = asking a question nobody has answered, designing a way to find an answer, and telling the world what you found.**

That's it. Everything else (the math, the code, the paper) is a tool to do those three things well.

---

## 🤔 Why Is Research Different From a School Project?

| School Project | Research |
|---|---|
| Question is given | Question is open — you find it |
| Methods are known | Methods are invented or combined |
| Right answer exists | The "best" answer is debated |
| One submission, done | Iterated for months or years |
| Graded by a teacher | Judged by peers and the community |
| Work stays with you | Work is **published** for the world |

The biggest shift: **nobody knows the answer**. That can feel scary — and that's normal.

---

## 🔬 The 3 Big Types Of Research

### 1. Basic (Pure) Research
**Goal:** Understand how something works. No immediate use.
- *Example:* "Why do transformers work better than RNNs on long sequences?"

### 2. Applied Research
**Goal:** Solve a real-world problem.
- *Example:* "Can we detect diabetic retinopathy in eye photos?"

### 3. Experimental Research
**Goal:** Test if a method works in practice.
- *Example:* "Does prompt-engineering LLM A beat LLM B on our benchmark?"

**Most MSc theses = applied + experimental.** You pick a real problem, try a method, measure, and report.

---

## 🔄 The Research Loop (the same one, every project)

```
         ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
         ↓                                    │
     1. Find a question                      │
         ↓                                    │
     2. Read what others did (literature)    │
         ↓                                    │
     3. Form a hypothesis                    │
         ↓                                    │
     4. Design an experiment (data + method) │
         ↓                                    │
     5. Run it, collect results              │
         ↓                                    │
     6. Analyze, interpret                   │
         ↓                                    │
     7. Write it up & share                  │
         ↓                                    │
     8. Get feedback → loop back to step 2   │
         └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

You'll repeat this loop **many times** in a thesis.

---

## 🧠 What Makes Research "Good"?

A good research project has:

| Quality | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Novel** | New question, new method, or new data |
| **Sound** | Methods are correct, results are reproducible |
| **Useful** | Someone (researcher, doctor, company) cares |
| **Clear** | Easy to understand, well-written |
| **Honest** | Negative results are reported too |

You don't need all 5 perfectly. **Sound + Honest = non-negotiable. The other 3 are what reviewers look for.**

---

## 🧩 Where Does Data Science Fit?

Data Science research usually combines:

| Layer | What |
|---|---|
| **Domain** | Healthcare, finance, climate, NLP, vision, etc. |
| **Data** | Real datasets (often messy) |
| **Method** | ML, DL, statistics, optimization |
| **Evaluation** | Metrics, baselines, ablations |
| **Insight** | What we learn that we didn't know before |

**Pick a domain you care about** — it will keep you going for 1-2 years.
