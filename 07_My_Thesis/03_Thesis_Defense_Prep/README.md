# Defense Preparation

> **The day you defend your thesis. Be ready.**

---

## What's A Defense?

You present your work to a committee (3-5 professors).
They ask you questions.
You answer.
You pass (or don't).

**Usually:** 15-20 min talk + 20-30 min questions.

---

## Your Presentation

### 15-20 slides
1. Title (1)
2. Problem (2-3)
3. Background (1-2)
4. Research questions (1)
5. Related work (2)
6. Your method (3-4)
7. Results (3-4)
8. Discussion (1-2)
9. Conclusion (1)
10. Future work (1)
11. Thanks (1)

### 10-20-30 Rule
- 10 slides
- 20 minutes
- 30-point font minimum

---

## 20 Likely Questions

Prepare answers for these:

1. Why is this problem important?
2. Why did you choose this method?
3. What are the limitations?
4. How is this better than [X]?
5. What dataset did you use and why?
6. Is your code public?
7. Can your work be reproduced?
8. What would you do differently?
9. How does this generalize?
10. What is the practical use?
11. Why these metrics?
12. Did you do ablation studies?
13. How much data did you have?
14. Were there ethical concerns?
15. What was the hardest part?
16. What if data changes?
17. Could industry use this?
18. How does this compare to baseline?
19. Did you try other methods?
20. What is your next step?

---

## Practice

- [ ] Practice 5+ times in front of friends
- [ ] Record yourself and watch
- [ ] Time yourself
- [ ] Practice Q&A with a fake committee
- [ ] Test your slides on the projector

---

## Day Of

- Dress formal
- Arrive 15 min early
- Bring water
- Bring backup of slides on USB
- Speak slowly
- Make eye contact
- Smile 😊
- If you don't know: "That's a great question. I'd need to think more about that."

---

## ✅ Done When

- [ ] Slides ready
- [ ] 20 questions answered
- [ ] 5 mock defenses done
- [ ] **You defended!** 🎓
